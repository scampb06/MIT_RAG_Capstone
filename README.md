# Capstone project for MIT RAG course

A retrieval-augmented question answering system over a corpus of 2,419 English Wikipedia articles, built checkpoint by checkpoint: from a no-retrieval baseline, through hybrid retrieval and an evaluation harness, to chunked, decomposed and graph-based advanced retrieval.

## Checkpoints

| Checkpoint | Script | Findings |
|---|---|---|
| **1.1** | [`capstone_checkpoint_1_1_baseline_solution.py`](Checkpoint%201.1/capstone_checkpoint_1_1_baseline_solution.py) establishes the no-retrieval baseline. It builds a plain LangChain chain over `openai/gpt-5.4-mini` via OpenRouter with no retrieval and no document parsing, runs the starter's example prompts plus six hand-written Wikipedia probes covering obscure facts, a direct quotation, a nonsense entity, a two-ceremony comparison and a post-cutoff event, and logs every prompt and response to `checkpoint_1_1_responses.log` so answers can be checked by hand against the real articles. | The model fabricates confidently whenever a question outruns its parametric memory. It invented a Wikipedia article for the non-existent "huba juice", produced a period-styled but fictitious quotation from Charlotte Brontë's 1846 letter and named the wrong publisher, gave Calton's 2008 male life expectancy as 53 rather than 54, named the wrong Best Supporting Actor presenters at both the 85th and 95th Academy Awards, and combined stale Apollo and Artemis facts into a false claim that a 2026 Moon landing was expected. Behaviour was inconsistent, with the same nonsense question sometimes drawing an honest refusal. Conclusion: retrieval is required to ground answers, but it will not stop hallucination on out-of-corpus or nonsensical questions unless the prompt confines the model to retrieved text. |
| **2.1** | [`capstone_checkpoint_2_1_baseline_retrieval_solution.py`](Checkpoint%202.1/capstone_checkpoint_2_1_baseline_retrieval_solution.py) implements the baseline hybrid retriever. `extract_wikipedia_text()` pulls plain text from each HTML article with trafilatura, `build_or_load_wikipedia_db()` embeds whole articles into a persistent Chroma store, and `WikipediaHybridRetriever` fuses BM25 keyword scores with vector similarity at equal weight after min-max normalisation, drawing from a candidate pool of 10 and passing the top 3 whole articles to the LLM. It then runs 15 test queries spanning representative and two rounds of adversarial cases, logging retrieval and answers to `checkpoint_2_1_retrieval.log`. | Hybrid retrieval handled single-article lookups well, correctly finding that the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake shortened the day by 1.8 microseconds even when the query wording differed from the article. It failed on multi-entity and multi-article questions: the 85th/95th Academy Awards comparison retrieved three award-category pages instead of the two ceremony articles and wrongly attributed *12 Years a Slave* to the 85th, and both Olympics questions retrieved thematically adjacent but wrong-year articles. Diagnosis: `TOP_K=3` over whole articles lets adjacent documents crowd out required ones, trafilatura flattens tables so their structure is lost, and on the Reagan/NFL query the model silently answered from parametric memory in violation of the system prompt. Proposed next steps were a higher top-k and candidate pool, title matching, splitting and chunking, richer extraction of headings, tables, wikilinks and infoboxes, and query decomposition. |
| **3.1** | [`capstone_checkpoint_3_1_evaluation_solution.py`](Checkpoint%203.1/capstone_checkpoint_3_1_evaluation_solution.py) is the evaluation harness. It keeps the 2.1 hybrid retriever and adds a golden-suite loader, three LLM judges (correctness with graded levels, aspect coverage and relevance; claim-level groundedness, citation support and chunk attribution measured against a closed-book answer; and refusal behaviour) plus deterministic retrieval, citation and quote metrics — 19 in total across retrieval, generation and operations. Results are written per record to JSON and CSV in `evaluation_results/`, with a summary printer, an `--input` flag to select a suite, and a `validate_framework()` check that the judge passes a correct answer and fails a manipulated one. | The baseline scored 12/18 (66.7%) correct on the generated suite and 9/15 (60%) on the handcrafted one, with mean precision of 53.7% and 31.1% respectively; four handcrafted records retrieved none of their required documents. Retrieval is the principal bottleneck — whole-article search at `TOP_K=3` gives a poor signal-to-noise ratio — but generation problems were more common than expected, including misattributed citations, unsupported claims and one answer drawn from parametric memory. The framework itself proved reliable and reproducible, catching a citation misattribution in Q011 and an unsupported editorial claim in Q094 across two independent runs, but it has blind spots: honest refusals are penalised as ungrounded because no document asserts an absence, and complete, well-cited answers were sometimes falsely flagged as hallucinated. Cost of about 15 cents per query exceeded the 10 cent budget, and latency needed percentile rather than mean reporting. Notably, the handcrafted queries exposed more weaknesses than the automatically generated suite. |
| **4.1** | [`capstone_checkpoint_4_1_advanced_retrieval_solution.py`](Checkpoint%204.1/capstone_checkpoint_4_1_advanced_retrieval_solution.py) adds advanced retrieval to the 3.1 harness with the judges and metrics carried over unchanged, so every configuration is scored by the same instruments. A `--retriever` switch selects four strategies behind a single seam: baseline whole-article fusion; chunks, retrieving over 134,107 chunks at top-k 8 from a candidate pool of 40; decompose, which splits a query into LLM sub-queries and sums fused scores per chunk; and graph, a NetworkX layer of 28,054 nodes and 56,761 edges that expands seed articles through category, infobox and hyperlink relations with primary versus context labelling. It also adds `--parent` expansion with a context budget, a resumable `--build-index`, a cost-free `--retrieval-only` mode, rate pacing with retries, incremental result saves, and chunk-aware metrics including evidence recall and matched-count recall over the first three articles. | All three advanced retrievers improved retrieval sharply — strict recall rose from 6% to 41%, 47% and 59% and evidence recall from 36% to about 50% — while context per query fell from 225k to 25–39k characters and cost from $3.00 to between $0.40 and $0.60 per 17 records. Correctness moved far less: 6/17 for chunks, 7/17 for decompose and 8/17 for graph against a baseline of 7/17, though graded correctness rose from 0.47 to 0.66 under the graph retriever. Each technique won where predicted and lost elsewhere: chunking fixed long-document dilution but its capped parent policy lost Q049; decomposition solved multi-entity comparisons such as Q003 but halved chunk attribution from 0.62 to 0.48 by naming answer entities in sub-queries from the model's own knowledge; and the graph alone solved the unnamed-entity records G08 and G13 while dropping precision to 26% through over-broad expansion. The remaining failures are mostly generation-side, and the proposed next step is an orchestrator that routes each query to the retriever best suited to it. |
